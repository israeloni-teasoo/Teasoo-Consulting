Tree4Life

Regreening Edo State — one million trees, restored forests and thriving communities.

Tree4Life is Seplat Energy's flagship reforestation programme in Edo State, Nigeria.

A pioneering restoration model that is regreening degraded forest reserve, rebuilding biodiversity and capturing carbon for generations to come.

It gives host communities direct access to green jobs, food trees and sustainable livelihoods.

Why is this project needed?

Degraded forests, a warming climate

Across Edo State, decades of logging and farming pressure have thinned once-dense reserves — eroding soils, displacing wildlife and releasing stored carbon. The communities that depend on the forest for food, income and shelter feel the loss first.

Trees are the solution. Restoring native forest rebuilds soil fertility, cools the land, shelters wildlife and draws carbon dioxide out of the atmosphere — a nature-based response to the climate crisis that also creates green livelihoods where they are needed most.

Our Aims

What every tree sets out to do

Over five years the programme will grow one million indigenous trees and rehabilitate 6,000 hectares of degraded forest reserve — sequestering carbon, restoring habitats and adding to the body of forestry knowledge through partnership with academia.

Access to land, training and income is improved for all members of host communities — including women and young people — ensuring the project's benefits are sustainable and long-term.

How the programme works

From strategy to standing forest

Every phase is planned across three disciplines — getting the science right, bringing communities in, and protecting what has been planted.

Strategic

Right species, right place

Species were chosen with the Edo State Forestry Commission and the University of Benin biodiversity team for growth rate, indigeneity and carbon capacity, guided by a concluded biodiversity and site-suitability study.

Operational

Communities at the centre

A 1.6 km farming buffer prevents encroachment, plantation guards are trained locally, and nurseries are timed to the March–October rains so saplings establish strongly.

Maintenance

Protecting every sapling

Round weeding clears competition around each seedling and fire-tracing rings the plantation — both concluded across the 2024 planting — while guards watch against grazing and trampling.

Indigenous species

Native trees, chosen for the reserve

Ten indigenous species were identified with the Edo State Forestry Commission. These lead the planting programme.

The five-year roadmap

Scaling from pilot to one million trees

Yearly planting targets step up as nurseries, community structures and monitoring mature — building to a fully restored 6,000 hectares.

1
30,000 trees

Phase 1 — Pilot

Biodiversity study, species selection and first plantings with core communities.

2
200,000 trees

Phase 2 — Scale-up

Nursery capacity and community programmes expand across the reserve.

3
280,000 trees

Phase 3 — Peak planting

Restoration reaches full annual pace with maturing survival systems.

4
280,000 trees

Phase 4 — Consolidation

Sustained planting alongside intensive maintenance of earlier phases.

5
210,000 trees

Phase 5 — Completion

Final plantings close the gap to one million trees and 6,000 hectares.
